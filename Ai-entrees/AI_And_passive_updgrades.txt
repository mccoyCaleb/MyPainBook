              <p>When your computer wants to send data to another device on the same LAN, it usually knows the destination IP address but not the destination MAC address.               <p>Think of it like this: the IP address is the street address, but the MAC address is the actual hardware identifier for the device on the local network. ARP connects those two layers so the packet can be delivered correctly.</p>
              <p>ARP is especially important on local networks because it helps devices communicate before traffic leaves the network segment.</p>
